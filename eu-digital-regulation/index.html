EU Digital Regulation

GDPR, ODD, DMA, DSA, DGA, Data Act, and AI Act on one page: what each covers and who must comply.

GDPR

General Data Protection Regulation
(2018)

Individual data protection.

Users must consent to use of their data.

Everyone doing business in Europe must comply.

ODD

Open Data Directive
(2019)

Public sector open data.

Standardized data should be made available for reuse.

EU nations must enact policies for all public sector agencies.

DMA

Digital Markets Act
(2023)

Digital antitrust.

Large platforms must be more transparent and compete openly cross-product.

Only applies to designated big tech Gatekeepers.

(Currently: Alphabet, Amazon, Apple, Booking, ByteDance, Meta, Microsoft)

DSA

Digital Services Act
(2024)

Protect fundamental rights online.

Companies must moderate content, have algorithm transparency, and combat misinformation.

Everyone doing business in Europe must comply.

(Small businesses excepted.)

DGA

Data Governance Act
(2023)

Promote closed data economy.

Public sector must facilitate the data market by providing off-the-shelf data products (for any non-ODD data).

Regulates data intermediaries as trusted data sharing businesses.

Recognizes social benefit data altruism organizations.

Data Act

(2025)

Broaden supplier choice.

Disallows lock-in for software on hardware (e.g. allows deletion of pre-installed apps).

Eases portability between cloud service providers.

AI Act

Artificial Intelligence Act
(2025)

Manage AI risks.

Companies must ensure responsible AI through risk assessment, ethical guidelines, and transparency.

Everyone using AI with users in Europe must comply.

Non-exhaustive comparison summary. Year represents first implementation deadline.
